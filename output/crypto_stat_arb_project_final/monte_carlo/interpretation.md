### Reading the Monte Carlo results

All figures below describe 625 simulated days from **100,000 USDT**, with the baseline 20 bps all-in trading costs already included. Extra slippage and borrow runs are sensitivity tests.

**Validation retrospective:** the reference ends at a median **116,961 USDT**, with a 5th–95th percentile range of **100,109–137,945 USDT**. It finishes below its starting capital in **4.9%** of paths and reaches a drawdown of at least 20% in **0.0%**. The loss-frequency Monte Carlo standard error is 0.14 percentage points; this measures simulation noise, not uncertainty about future market conditions.

**Walk-forward retrospective:** the reference ends at a median **116,739 USDT**, with a 5th–95th percentile range of **89,666–155,193 USDT**. It finishes below its starting capital in **17.0%** of paths and reaches a drawdown of at least 20% in **8.6%**. The loss-frequency Monte Carlo standard error is 0.24 percentage points; this measures simulation noise, not uncertainty about future market conditions.

**BTC hedge, validation retrospective:** median terminal capital is **118,522 USDT**; the terminal-loss frequency is **3.1%**. These results are displayed for comparison and do not replace the nominated reference portfolio.

Validation and walk-forward distributions are retrospective descriptions of their observed return regimes. Weaker walk-forward outcomes or large changes across block lengths indicate sensitivity to the source history; no simulated frequency is a guarantee, an independent validation test, or the probability that alpha is real.
